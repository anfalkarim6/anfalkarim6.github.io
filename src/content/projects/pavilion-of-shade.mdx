## A courtyard with no shade

Our school courtyard is bare brick, and between May and September nobody sits in it after ten in the morning. Four of us proposed a pavilion to the principal, with a budget of whatever the art department could spare.

## Shade you can see

We chose bamboo because it is cheap, light, and sold near the school, and because four teenagers could carry it. The roof is not solid: it is made of slats spaced 250 mm apart. A solid roof would have been darker and hotter underneath, because it would trap heat. The slats let hot air escape upward while still blocking most of the direct sun.

The best part is something we didn't plan: the shadow is a set of stripes that slowly rotates across the brick as the sun moves. Younger students started using it to tell the time.

## Building it

We tested joints for two weekends before choosing lashed jute over bolts; bolts split the bamboo. The pavilion took six Saturdays to build. It has survived one Quetta winter so far, with two slats replaced.

## What I took from it

Drawing a building and building a building are different skills. On paper every slat is identical; in reality every piece of bamboo is a slightly different shape, and the design has to absorb that. I now leave tolerance in my drawings for the world to be imperfect.
