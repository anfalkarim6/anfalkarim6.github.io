## The brief

Our summer studio was given a plot just inside Bhati Gate: twelve metres wide, almost windowless on three sides, surrounded by four- and five-storey houses. The brief asked for a public building for the neighbourhood. I chose a reading room, because the nearest public library is a bus ride away and the lanes around the plot are full of children after school.

## Light from above

With no windows possible on the sides, every bit of light had to come from the roof. I studied Kahn's Kimbell Art Museum and his assembly in Dhaka, and borrowed his trick: a slot at the top of each vault, with a reflector hanging underneath it, so the hard Lahore sun is turned into soft light that washes down the curved ceiling.

I tested the idea with a 1:50 card model under a desk lamp, moving the lamp to the angle of the sun at noon in June and in December. The reflector went through five versions. The fourth was too solid and made the room gloomy; the fifth is perforated, so a little direct light leaks through as a pattern of dots on the floor, like a jaali.

## Shelves as structure

The bookshelves line the thick party walls on both levels, so the walls the old city gave me become the library itself. A mezzanine runs along the west side for older readers; the ground floor stays open for children, tables and a stair wide enough to sit on.

## What I would change

My tutor's main criticism was that the building is beautiful inside and silent outside: from the lane you only see a door. If I did it again I would bring one vault down to street level as a deep, shaded porch, so that the reading room announces itself to the neighbourhood instead of hiding from it.
