## Why Multan

Multan regularly passes 45 °C in June. Most new houses there are concrete boxes cooled by air conditioners that fail whenever the power does. I wanted to see how far I could get with the oldest tools in the region: shade, mass, air movement and water.

## Courtyard and verandah

The house is planned around a paved courtyard, the *sehn*, with a neem tree and a shallow pool. On its north side is a deep verandah, the *dalaan*. I worked out the sun angles for Multan's latitude: at noon the summer sun is almost overhead, at about 83°, and the verandah roof keeps it off the inner wall. In winter the sun is low, at about 36°, and reaches right into the rooms behind.

## The wind catcher

On the roof of the main bedroom is a *mangh*, a wind catcher of the kind that once crowded the skyline of Hyderabad in Sindh. It faces the prevailing breeze and drops it down a shaft into the room. Warm air rises out through the courtyard, which pulls more cool air in behind it.

## What I learned

None of these ideas are mine; every one of them is centuries old. What was new for me was measuring them: putting numbers on the sun angles and the overhangs made the old house stop being nostalgic and start being a design method.
