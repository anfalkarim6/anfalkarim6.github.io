The Kaufmann family owned a stretch of woodland in Pennsylvania with a stream and a waterfall where they liked to swim. In 1935 they asked Frank Lloyd Wright to design a weekend house with a view of the falls.

Wright did not give them a view of the falls. He built the house *on* them.

## Drawn in an afternoon

The story goes that Wright did almost nothing on the design for months. Then Edgar Kaufmann phoned to say he was driving over to see the drawings, and Wright sat down and drew the whole house in the few hours it took him to arrive.

It is probably exaggerated, but I believe the important part: by the time he drew it, he had spent months thinking about the site. The drawing was fast because the looking had been slow.

## Anchored in the rock

The house is built around a boulder where the family used to sunbathe. Wright kept it, and it comes up through the living-room floor to become the hearth. From the same room, a hanging stair drops through a hatch straight down to the stream.

The famous terraces are reinforced concrete cantilevers, anchored into the rock and the house's stone core and reaching out over the water with nothing underneath them.<Note n={1}>The colours were chosen carefully too. The concrete is a pale ochre and the window frames are a deep red that Wright used often. In autumn the house matches the leaves.</Note>

## The sag

The engineers were worried from the start. They added more steel than Wright wanted, and Wright was furious. Even so, the cantilevers began to sag almost as soon as the formwork came out, and they kept slowly sagging for decades.

In 2002, engineers finally strengthened the main cantilevers with steel cables tensioned inside the structure. The house is safe now. But for more than sixty years, the most famous house in America was quietly losing an argument with gravity.

## What I learned

I used to think the lesson of Fallingwater was boldness: put the house where no one else would. Now I think the lesson is attention. Wright paid attention to a boulder, a stream, the colour of autumn leaves. Where he stopped paying attention (to what concrete can actually do), the house started to fail.

So: be bold about the idea, and humble about the materials. Or at least listen to your engineer.
