# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: a public readership of people curious about buildings: architecture enthusiasts, other students, design-minded readers arriving from a shared link, usually on a phone in the evening or on a laptop at a desk. They come to read one essay and should leave wanting to read the next.

Secondary: admissions readers at architecture schools in the US and UK, who may follow a link from an application and judge the student's curiosity, visual literacy and voice in a few minutes.

The site belongs to a single student (pre-university, applying to architecture programmes for 2027 entry) who publishes essays on different architectural traditions and shows her own design projects. She is the only author; content changes when she publishes a new essay or project.

## Product Purpose

A personal publication and portfolio. It exists to (1) publish her essays on architecture, from Mughal screens to Brutalist estates, in a form that is a pleasure to read, (2) show her own design work as drawings, and (3) make it obvious within seconds that this person thinks like an architect. Success: readers finish an essay and open a second one; admissions readers remember her.

## Brand Personality

Bold, exacting, curious. The site should feel like walking into a studio crit wall at midnight: drawings pinned edge to edge, cyanotype blue everywhere, a voice that is confident without being academic. Emotional goals: wonder at buildings, respect for craft, the sense of a young architect already working.

## Anti-references

- Generic personal-blog templates (Medium clones, Notion-ish minimal blogs, Squarespace portfolio grids).
- "Blueprint theme" clichés: neon-cyan grid on navy with a monospace font everywhere and no real drawing content.
- Editorial-magazine affectation (italic display serif, drop caps, broadsheet rules) as the default look.
- SaaS landing-page grammar: hero metrics, icon-card grids, numbered section eyebrows.
- Cream/beige "paper" backgrounds.

## Design Principles

1. **Draw, don't decorate.** Every line on the page should read as a real architectural drawing convention (grid bubbles, level markers, dimension strings, title blocks, redlines), never as generic tech decoration.
2. **The essay is the building.** Long-form reading gets the calmest, most legible surface on the site; boldness lives on covers, indexes and transitions.
3. **Structure tells the truth.** Sheet numbers, revision tables and indexes encode real information about the content, not ornament.
4. **One bold move per view.** Each screen has one dominant idea; everything around it is quiet.
5. **Her hand is visible.** Projects are shown as her drawings, essays in her voice; nothing should feel like stock.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 on every surface (including on the cyanotype blue). All motion (line drawing, cursor crosshair, page transitions) has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback and is never the only carrier of meaning. Full keyboard navigation with visible focus. Images carry descriptive alt text; photo credits are given for every sourced image.
